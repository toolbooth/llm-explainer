# Letter kit — telling us you taught with this

*Inside the Machine: Classroom Edition · shared front matter · draft 2026-08-22. English only. Nothing in this kit is required to use the lessons.*

## Why we ask, and what we never ask for

The lessons collect nothing — no analytics, no accounts, no usage counts — so the only way we can ever know they were used is if a teacher tells us [PRODUCT.md §8, §9 "Adoption is invisible"]. Evidence of teaching use matters for three things, and we say all three plainly:

1. **Improving the lessons.** "What worked / what didn't" from a real class is the only feedback loop we have.
2. **Academic submissions.** A submission to the *Journal of Open Source Education* (JOSE) and conference posters need documented teaching use by instructors other than the author.
3. **The author's own professional and immigration records.** Letters describing real classroom use are evidence of the work's impact. You are being told this because you should know every use before you decide.

You can consent to any subset of those three, or none.

What we will **never** ask for, accept or store: student names, student work, screenshots that show students, exit-ticket responses, or any student-level data. If a teacher sends any of these anyway, we delete them and say so [PRODUCT.md §8.1]. We do not solicit student testimonials, and we do not offer gift cards, swag, early access or anything else in return. Any statement can be withdrawn at any time by emailing the address at the bottom; we remove it and confirm.

## Two ways to tell us, both optional

**A. The "I taught with this" report (public, two minutes).** Post in the repository's GitHub Discussions using the template, or email the same fields. Fields: your name (or "anonymous HS teacher, Ohio"), institution (optional), course, grade band, date(s), which modules, approximate class size rounded to tens, device type, one thing that worked, one thing that didn't [PRODUCT.md §8.2].

**B. A letter (only if you want to help further).** If you have posted a report and ask how else you can help, we send this kit. A letter is signed by you, on your letterhead if you choose, addressed "To whom it may concern," and describes your own use in your own words. We do not draft it for you beyond the skeleton below, and we do not edit what you send.

## What counts as "teaching use" — for JOSE and for us

For a *Journal of Open Source Education* submission, and for our own records, we treat a module as having been **used in teaching** only when all three of the following hold [PRODUCT.md §8.4]:

1. An instructor **other than the author** delivered it in a real course section — not a demo, not a webinar; and
2. that instructor documented the delivery in their own words (a report or a letter) naming institution, course, term, modules used and an approximate student count; and
3. the instructor gave explicit permission for the documentation to be cited.

Sessions the author runs — Hour of AI events, workshops, conference demos — count as **piloting** and are reported separately under that label. Our target before a JOSE submission is five instructors across three states or two countries, at least two modules, and at least one community college: enough to show it was not one friendly teacher, few enough to be achievable in a school year. (JOSE's author guidelines and reviewer checklist change; the current ones are checked at submission time.)
